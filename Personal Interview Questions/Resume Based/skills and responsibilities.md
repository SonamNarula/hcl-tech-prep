### Question 37 — You're the DSA Captain at Devcrest JU, a 400+ member community. What does that role actually involve?
**Difficulty:** Easy | **Category:** Position of Responsibility

**Interview Answer**
"As DSA Captain, I lead the DSA vertical for the community — designing the problem-solving curriculum/roadmap for members, running or organizing DSA sessions or contests, and mentoring members who are earlier in their DSA journey. With 400+ members, part of the role is also making content and guidance scalable rather than 1-on-1 for everyone."

**Follow-up Questions**
- How did you get selected for this role?
- What's a concrete initiative you personally drove in this role?
- How do you measure whether your DSA sessions/content are actually helping members improve?
- How do you handle members at very different skill levels in the same community?

**Common Mistake**
Describing the role only in title terms ("I lead DSA stuff") without a concrete example of something you actually built, organized, or improved — interviewers want evidence of impact, not just a title.

**Strong Candidate Point**
Have one specific number or outcome ready if possible — e.g., a session you ran, a roadmap you designed, a number of members who engaged with something you built.

### Question 39 — Tell me about your ICPC Algo Queen 2026 performance.
**Difficulty:** Medium | **Category:** Competition

**Interview Answer**
"I competed in ICPC Algo Queen 2026 and finished rank 386 out of 1,063 participants, solving 5 out of 6 problems. It was a good test of solving under time pressure across a range of difficulty."

**Follow-up Questions**
- What was the one problem you couldn't solve — what was your approach, and where did it break down?
- Which of the 5 you solved was the hardest, and what was your approach?
- How did you prepare for this contest specifically?
- What would you do differently next time?

**Common Mistake**
Not being able to discuss the actual problems — if you list a competition on your resume/profile, you should be able to describe at least one problem's approach in real technical detail. Be ready to actually explain your solution to at least the hardest problem you solved.

**Strong Candidate Point**
Being able to articulate *why* the 6th problem didn't get solved (e.g., "I had the right approach but ran out of time on implementation" vs "I didn't see the pattern") shows self-awareness about your problem-solving process, not just the result.

### Question 40 — You have 1,200+ problems solved across platforms and are Knight tier on LeetCode. What does that actually represent?
**Difficulty:** Easy | **Category:** DSA Practice / Credibility Check

**Interview Answer**
"Knight tier on LeetCode roughly means being in the top tier of contest performers — it reflects consistent contest participation and rating, not just problems solved casually. Combined with 1,200+ problems across platforms, it reflects sustained, structured practice across topics — arrays, DP, graphs, trees — rather than just grinding easy problems."

**Follow-up Questions**
- Pick any topic — how would you rate your strength in it, and why?
- Can you solve a medium-level array/string problem live right now?
- What's a problem that took you multiple attempts to actually solve — walk me through your thought process across those attempts.

**Common Mistake**
Treating "problems solved" as the impressive metric on its own — interviewers care much more about whether you can solve a *new* problem live than how many old ones you've seen before. Be ready to actually solve something in real time.

### Question 41 — Why are you doing a DSA+CP tracker spreadsheet with 1,037 problems? What does that show about how you work?
**Difficulty:** Easy | **Category:** Self-Management / Behavioral Bridge

**Interview Answer**
"I built it because I wanted structured visibility into my own progress — which topics I'd covered, which needed revisiting, and how consistent I was being day to day, rather than just solving problems ad hoc and losing track. It reflects how I like to work generally — I prefer having a system I can see progress in, rather than just trusting that I'm improving."

**Follow-up Questions**
- What did the tracker actually reveal about your weak areas?
- How do you decide what to revisit vs. move on from?

**Strong Candidate Point**
This is a good bridge into behavioral answers about self-discipline, planning, and taking ownership of your own learning — useful to connect explicitly if asked about strengths or work style.

### Question 42 — You're a final-year B.Tech CSE student. Tell me about your academic background.
**Difficulty:** Easy | **Category:** Academic

**Interview Answer**
"I'm a final-year B.Tech CSE student at JECRC University, Jaipur, graduating in 2027. Alongside coursework, I've focused heavily on DSA/competitive programming and, more recently, AI/ML — building projects and following a structured self-designed learning path rather than relying only on classroom content."

**Follow-up Questions**
- What's your CGPA/percentage? (Be ready with the actual number.)
- What was your favorite subject and why?
- What subject did you struggle with, and how did you handle it?
- Any backlogs or academic gaps you need to explain?

**Common Mistake**
Being caught off guard by the CGPA question — always know your exact number and be ready to contextualize it honestly if it's not particularly high (e.g., "I focused heavily on practical projects and DSA alongside coursework, which is reflected more in my project depth and problem-solving than pure GPA").

### Question 43 — Why are you learning German and targeting Germany for your career, and how is that relevant to this interview?
**Difficulty:** Medium | **Category:** Resume/Profile Cross-Question — HANDLE CAREFULLY

**Interview Answer**
"[This is a sensitive one — HCLTech interviewers may or may not know about this depending on what's visible on LinkedIn/GitHub. If asked directly, be honest but reframe around commitment, not exit-intent:] Long-term, I'm interested in international opportunities and have been learning German as part of that broader goal — but that's a multi-year horizon, and right now my focus is building strong fundamentals and real-world experience, which is exactly what I'm looking for in this role."

**Common Mistake**
Volunteering this information unprompted, or sounding like you're already planning to leave the company shortly after joining — this can genuinely hurt an offer decision. Only address it if directly asked, and frame the current role as valuable *regardless* of the long-term plan, not as a stepping stone you'll discard quickly.

**Follow-up Questions**
- How long do you see yourself staying with HCLTech if hired?
- Would relocation abroad happen during your time here, or is this a much longer-term plan?

**Strong Candidate Point**
If pressed, honestly frame it as: skills and experience gained at HCLTech are valuable and portable regardless of where your career eventually goes — this isn't dishonest, it's true, and it reframes the answer around value-add rather than short tenure risk.

### Question 45 — Why should I believe your GitHub/portfolio reflects real skill and not just following tutorials?
**Difficulty:** Hard | **Category:** Credibility / Trick Question

**Interview Answer**
"Happy to walk through any repo live and explain design decisions, not just what the code does — the difference between following a tutorial and understanding something is being able to explain *why* a particular approach was chosen and what you'd do differently. I can also point to places where I diverged from a standard approach because it didn't fit my specific requirements."

**Follow-up Questions**
- Pick one repo — explain a decision you made that a tutorial wouldn't have told you to make.
- What's something in your GitHub that you built completely from scratch, without following any guide?
